How DhabaGo earns

Four ways the app makes money · visible to admins only

How a food order splits

No delivery riders: the passenger walks to the counter when the bus stops. That keeps most of every rupee as margin.

Today so far

1

Commission from dhabas

10–15% per order · main income

Take about 10–15% of every order the dhaba gets through the app. That is lower than Zomato or Swiggy, and it is easy to justify: DhabaGo brings customers the dhaba would otherwise lose.

Today a dhaba can only serve as many people as it can cook for in a 10–20 minute halt. Pre-orders let it sell more in that same window.

2

Small fee from passengers

₹5–10 per order

A convenience fee on every order, now shown at checkout. Passengers pay it because they skip the queue and their food is ready when the bus stops.

3

Advertising

Charged per day or per tap
  • Sponsored dhabas: a dhaba pays to be pinned at the top of its stop with an offer.
  • Trip banner: brands pay to show ads to passengers on a specific route.
  • Menu add-ons: a water or snack brand pays to appear inside every menu.
  • Order wait screen: an ad while the passenger waits for food.

Highway travellers are a captive audience, which brands like.

4

Add-on sales and referrals

Margin on extras + referral commission
  • Sell water, snacks or travel kits with orders and keep the margin.
  • Earn a referral commission on travel insurance or bus-ticket partners, like SafarCover in the demo.
